Two Projects, Two Design Approaches

Together, these projects demonstrate two different approaches to instructional design. Leadership Readiness focuses on scenario-based decision-making and deeper Storyline 360 development, while NexaTech explores structured Rise 360 course architecture, onboarding design, and the learner journey across a broader program.

Leadership Readiness Pathway

Scenario-Based Learning · Storyline 360

Primary Focus

Interactive leadership scenarios that allow learners to practise judgment, make decisions, experience consequences, and reflect on their choices.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Storyline 360 development
  • Branching scenario design
  • Variables and triggers
  • Consequence-based feedback
  • Path-dependent interaction
  • Personalised learner reflection
  • Scenario-based assessment
  • Higher-complexity decision design

What This Project Demonstrates

Leadership Readiness demonstrates deeper exploration of interactive Storyline development. Each module is designed around scenario-based practice, with Storyline used to create branching interactions, store learner choices, control consequences, and support personalised reflection.

NexaTech Onboarding Pathway

Course Architecture · Rise 360

Primary Focus

A structured onboarding experience focused on organising learning content, sequencing modules, guiding learners through the program, and creating a cohesive Rise 360 experience.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Rise 360 course development
  • Curriculum architecture
  • Module sequencing
  • Onboarding experience design
  • Content organisation
  • Learner navigation
  • Lesson and interaction structure
  • Visual experience design in Rise

What This Project Demonstrates

NexaTech demonstrates broader exploration of Rise 360 as a course-development environment, with emphasis on structuring the learner journey, organising onboarding content, sequencing modules, and creating a cohesive learning experience across the program.

Design Approach

I design for what learners need to do, not just what they need to know. My approach combines the structure of curriculum design with the practical focus of workplace learning.

01

Start with the Learning Problem

Identify the performance need before deciding on the learning solution.

02

Design for Practice

Give learners opportunities to apply judgment, make decisions, and work through realistic situations.

03

Make Interaction Meaningful

Use branching, feedback, consequences, and reflection when they support the learning goal.

My Design Process

I connect learning needs, instructional strategy, and development throughout the design process.

01 · DISCOVER

Understand

Identify the audience, performance need, and learning goals.

02 · DESIGN

Structure

Build the learning architecture, objectives, scenarios, and storyboard.

03 · DEVELOP

Build

Turn the design into an interactive learning experience using Rise 360 and Storyline 360.

04 · REFLECT

Refine

Review the learner experience and refine the design based on what the interaction is meant to accomplish.
